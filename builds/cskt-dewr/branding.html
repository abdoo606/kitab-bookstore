Three Initial Branding Concepts

Addendum #1 Q27 asks for three starting directions rather than one fixed logo. Each concept below explores a different answer to the same question — how the Department of Natural Resources, its three divisions, and DEWR's water work should relate visually — and each is drawn as original vector geometry that can be rebuilt by any designer.

Select a concept to mark it for the shortlist (demo only; nothing is saved or sent).

Concept A

Unified NRD Identity

One department mark, DEWR carried in the name line.

Treats the Department of Natural Resources as the single public brand and places DEWR, FWRC, and Environmental Protection beneath it as a consistent division name line. Fewest marks to maintain and one predictable place for the official Seal.

Best for
Maximum consistency and the lowest ongoing design-maintenance cost for DEWR staff.
Trade-off
DEWR's individual program identity is quieter; division recognition depends on the name line staying readable at small sizes.

Shared grammar, three distinct division marks.

Concept C

Watershed & Water Compact System

Contour lines and a channel line carrying the Compact story.

Abstract topographic contours and a meandering channel line express the Flathead watershed and cooperative water stewardship without borrowing any sacred or ceremonial imagery. Division names sit in one flexible band, so a fourth program can be added later without a new mark.

Best for

How the three concepts are evaluated and chosen

  • Each concept is presented as a short rationale, a best-fit use case, an honest trade-off, and a palette — not as a finished brand.
  • DEWR reviewers compare them on a shared checklist: recognisability at 24px, legibility in one colour, behaviour beside the official Seal, and how easily DEWR staff can apply it without a designer.
  • The chosen direction is refined through two structured revision rounds before the full style guide and templates are locked.
  • Nothing is adopted, published, or placed on merchandise until the Culture Committee review completes in writing.

Official CSKT Seal — Reserved Placement Zone Only

Addendum #1 Q27 and Q28. The Seal is a sovereign emblem. This prototype reserves the space and enforces the rules; it never imitates the artwork.

Reserved
official CSKT
Seal placement

Empty by design. No seal drawing, trace, recolour, or approximation exists in this codebase.

Placement rules carried into the CMS

  • Only the official artwork file supplied by DEWR is used — never a screenshot, a redraw, or a colour-sampled copy.
  • Approved locations: site footer, the About the Tribes page, and formal public-notice documents.
  • No seal on social graphics, merchandise, program badges, or watermark positions without written authorisation.
  • Minimum clear space of 1× the seal's height on every side, with no text overlay, gradient, or photograph behind it.
  • Minimum on-screen size defined in the style guide so the seal never renders as an illegible thumbnail.
  • Alt text is fixed and approved by DEWR, not improvised per page.

Cultural review gate (Q28)

  • All concepts, colours, motifs, photographs, and language strings go to DEWR first.
  • DEWR routes them to the CSKT Culture Committees; I never contact Committees, Elders, or Tribal members directly.
  • No culturally sensitive imagery, plant knowledge, or ceremony reference is published before written approval.
  • Approval status is tracked per asset in the CMS: Draft → DEWR review → Culture Committee review → Approved.

Why this gate is non-negotiable in the schedule

Culture Committee review is a dependency, not a formality. Phase 1 therefore ends with an approved visual direction rather than a finished brand, and Phase 2 templates are built against approved assets only. If review needs more time, the milestone waits — I will never publish first and ask later, and I will not treat a delay in review as a billable change.

Salish & Ksanka Typography Support

Addendum #1 Q29. Both languages need characters that ordinary Latin fonts drop or mangle, so support is treated as a technical requirement rather than a decorative nicety.

Salish · Séliš / Ql̓ispé

Séliš · Ql̓ispé · Nɫawqin

Glottal stop (ʼ) and barred-L (ɫ) preserved as true Unicode, not ASCII lookalikes.

Ksanka · Ktunaxa

Ktunaxa

Placeholder for font-coverage testing only. Actual wording and orthography are supplied and approved by DEWR and the Culture Committees — never invented by the contractor.

Encoding

UTF-8 end to end

The database, the WordPress editor, the search index, the sitemap, and every PDF export use UTF-8 with no truncation. Character encoding is verified with real strings, not with English test copy.

Screen readers

Correct language attributes

Salish content is marked lang="sal" and Ktunaxa content lang="kut", so assistive technology switches pronunciation rules instead of reading the words as English.

Fonts

Licensed before embedded

No webfont is purchased, self-hosted, or embedded until DEWR confirms licensing. Until then a Unicode-complete fallback stack renders the characters correctly, so nothing breaks in the meantime.

Testing

Marks survive zoom and print

Combining diacritics are checked at 200% zoom, in print, and at small sizes for both screen and the printed style guide.

Imagery Policy — Zero Stock, Zero AI Landscapes

Addendum #1 Q35. This is a copyright and cultural-integrity rule as much as a design preference.

What the prototype does

  • Every illustration is original inline SVG geometry authored for DEWR.
  • No stock photograph, no AI-generated landscape, and no scraped image exists in the build.
  • Photo slots are labelled placeholders that state exactly what DEWR must supply and who must approve it.
  • Icons are simple geometric shapes, not traced cultural symbols.

What the live site will do

  • Use DEWR-supplied photographs with a recorded source, permission, and photographer credit.
  • Record the rights basis for each image in the media library fields, so nobody has to remember later.
  • Keep a written release note where a recognisable person appears.
  • Refuse AI-generated imagery of land, water, people, or cultural material — full stop.
  • No stock photo subscriptions passed to DEWR as a hidden cost.
  • No AI image generator in the content workflow.
