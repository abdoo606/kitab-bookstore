Visitor Guide

Before you go

Weather changes fast on the Trail. Carry navigation, shelter and emergency communication; tell someone your plan. (Final safety copy from the Alliance.)

Respect communities & lands

The route crosses public, Native corporation and private lands. Follow local guidance, pack out everything, and support local businesses.

Winter travel

Winter is the Trail's historic season — and its most demanding. Check conditions, river ice advisories and community resources before setting out.

Recreation Info

Summer & fall

Hiking, mountain biking, horseback riding and heritage touring on southern segments and community trails.

Winter & spring

Dog mushing, skiing, snowmachining and fat-biking — including the human-powered and sled-dog races the Trail is famous for.

Passport Program

Collect stamps at participating locations along the Trail and track your journey from Seward toward Nome. A family-friendly way to discover gateway communities one stop at a time.

Places to Visit

Mile 1 — Seward

Where the historic route begins: waterfront trailheads, museums and the original survey country.

Trail communities

Vibrant rural communities along the route welcome respectful visitors — fuel, food, stories and local knowledge.

Nome
